Logo directions

Four ways to build a wordmark from your existing palette and typography. No new fonts, single sage accent, optional botanical mark drawn from a single cannabis leaflet (not the dispensary cliché).

Option A

Magazine masthead

"Bloomy's" treated as a byline in italic, "BUD LOG" as the primary title in tracked caps. A hairline rule between. Feels like the front of a quarterly journal. No icon at all — relies entirely on typography and structure. Strongest if you want to read as a publication rather than a tool.

Option B

Single leaflet + wordmark

A botanical-illustration cannabis leaflet (one of the seven, not the whole leaf) sits left of an italic Fraunces wordmark. Reads as "cannabis" to anyone who knows, reads as "botanical brand" to anyone who doesn't. Works tiny — the leaflet alone makes a clean favicon or avatar.

Option C

Inline with botanical fleuron

"Bloomy's" and "Bud Log" run as a single italic phrase separated by a small fleuron — the printer's-ornament tradition used by literary mastheads (think The Paris Review). The fleuron here is a custom mirrored leaflet pair. Light, refined, very editorial. Works as one continuous mark.

Option D

Monogram + wordmark

A circular monogram — italic Fraunces B with a small leaflet tucked in the lower-right quadrant — paired with the full wordmark. Most versatile: the monogram works as a standalone mark for favicons, app icons, profile pictures, business cards. Borrows from heritage logo conventions (think apothecary or distillery seals).
